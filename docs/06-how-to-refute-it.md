# How UDL could be proved wrong

A hypothesis earns attention by naming the results that would kill it.

## 1. Gravitationally induced entanglement (refutes H5, H6)

**Proposal:** Bose et al. and Marletto & Vedral (2017). Put two microscopic masses, each in a superposition of two positions, close enough that they interact only through gravity. Then check whether they became entangled.

- **Entangled:** gravity can carry quantum information, so it has a quantum nature. H5 and H6 in their current form are refuted.
- **Not entangled** (with enough sensitivity): strong support for classical gravity, and for UDL's H5.

**Status:** not yet feasible. Needs masses around 10⁻¹⁴ kg held in superposition for about a second. Active experimental programmes work toward it.

## 2. Gravitational noise (supports H5 if seen)

Oppenheim's classical gravity predicts a minimum amount of randomness in gravitational fields, and so a trade-off: the better a mass stays in superposition, the noisier its gravitational field must be. Precise mass measurements (for example, repeated weighings of the kilogram standard) already constrain it. Seeing this noise would support H5; ruling out the whole allowed range would refute it.

## 3. Bell tests (H4 already passed)

Any version of UDL where entangled particles carry stored values is refuted by the loophole-free Bell tests of 2015 (Delft, Vienna, NIST) and by the experiments recognised with the 2022 Nobel Prize. The current version (shared inode, random commit) passes, as the simulation shows.

## 4. Finite capacity (H1, open problem 2)

If the lake has finite capacity per area, there may be limits on how much entanglement or how many superposed branches a region can hold. No such limit has been seen. A precise UDL bound that fails tests would refute that version.

## 5. Speed of the commit

If the commit in H4 had a finite speed, however large, there would be a distance beyond which entangled correlations fail. Experiments have bounded any such "speed of spooky action" to at least 10⁴ times the speed of light (Salart et al. 2008; Yin et al. 2013). A UDL with finite commit speed is only allowed above those bounds.
