# Open problems

Listed roughly from most to least serious.

### 1. No new prediction yet
Everything UDL says about quantum phenomena reproduces standard quantum mechanics, by design. That makes it an *interpretation*, like Copenhagen or Bohmian mechanics. To become a theory it must say where it *differs*. Candidates: finite speed of the commit (H4), finite lake capacity limiting how many systems can be entangled, or stochastic gravity (H5).

### 2. Is the lake continuous or finite?
The original intuition was that the lake is *analogue* (continuous), since electromagnetic waves and wave functions are continuous. But continuity of an equation is a property of the description, not necessarily of reality. And the Bekenstein bound and black-hole entropy say a finite region holds finite information, proportional to the area of its boundary (S = A/4 in Planck units, roughly one bit per Planck area). A truly continuous lake would hold infinite information. Possible resolutions: the lake is finite and the continuity is an approximation; or the lake is continuous but only finitely *readable*. Either needs work.

### 3. Discreteness is not digitization
The first version compared quantum discreteness to a DAC/ADC. A better analogy is a guitar string: a continuous object that, because it is bounded, can only ring at certain frequencies. Atomic energy levels are discrete for this reason. UDL should adopt this mechanism ("reads with boundary conditions") instead of sampling.

### 4. Non-locality
The commit in H4 acts instantly at any distance. That is allowed (it carries no signal), and other interpretations share it, but it makes the "lake" a strange object with no notion of distance for commits. Combined with relativity, the model must explain why the order of commits never matters.

### 5. Gravity of superpositions
See [04](04-gravity.md). UDL has to commit to a mechanism, and every mechanism is constrained by experiment.

### 6. What exactly is a "read"?
The measurement problem in new words: which interactions count as reads? If any interaction does, why do interference experiments with large molecules (about two thousand atoms) still work? Decoherence theory gives a partial answer that UDL could adopt.

### 7. Where do the weights come from?
Why complex numbers, and why probability = |weight|²? Standard quantum mechanics postulates both. If UDL could derive them from properties of a passive store, that would be a real result.
