# Gravity as a property of the lake

## The claim

Quantum theory describes what happens when the lake is *read*. Gravity describes the lake *itself*. If that is right, gravity is not one more quantum field, and quantizing it, the decades-long programme of quantum gravity, may be asking the wrong question.

## Serious work in the same direction

- **Jacobson (1995)** derived Einstein's field equations from thermodynamics: assume each small patch of spacetime obeys entropy ∝ area, and general relativity follows as an *equation of state*, like the gas laws. Gravity then is a macroscopic property of some underlying medium, as UDL proposes.
- **Verlinde (2011)** proposed entropic gravity, with gravity as an entropic force arising from information associated with positions of matter.
- **Analogue gravity** (Unruh 1981 onwards) shows that horizons, Hawking-type radiation and curved-spacetime wave equations arise in ordinary media: water, light in optical fibre, Bose–Einstein condensates. Spacetime-like geometry demonstrably *can* emerge from a substrate. The [water experiment](../experiments/water-horizon/) in this repository is a home version.
- **Oppenheim (2023)**, postquantum classical gravity: a consistent theory in which spacetime stays classical while matter is quantum. The price is fundamental randomness in gravity, which makes testable predictions (see [06](06-how-to-refute-it.md)).

## The hard problem: gravity of a superposition

Gravity is sourced by mass-energy, and mass belongs to quantum objects. Take a mass in a superposition of "here" and "there", one inode with two links. What gravitational field does the lake have?

- **It follows the average** (semiclassical gravity). Page and Geilker (1981) tested this and the simplest version failed.
- **It follows one link at random.** That is close to Oppenheim's approach: the lake "reads" matter stochastically, adding a little noise and slowly decohering superpositions.
- **It follows both** (gravity is quantum). Then H5 is wrong.

UDL must pick the second option, or something new. That choice is what makes it testable.

## Where the lake's geometry comes from

Not yet answered. Candidates consistent with UDL:

- **Density of stored information.** Geometry reflects how much information a region holds, close to the holographic picture and to Jacobson.
- **Link structure.** Distance reflects how strongly regions are entangled with each other (the "ER = EPR" and "spacetime from entanglement" programme of Van Raamsdonk, Maldacena and Susskind). In UDL language: space is the link graph of the lake. This would tie H4 and H5 together, but it is in tension with H6, since in that programme gravity is fundamentally quantum.
